# Probe vault inspection: adding a photographic baseline

Change to how the inspection round is run, agreed with Meridian's maintenance
lead after the spring cycle.

The round now opens with a fixed photograph set at every chamber, taken before
anything is touched: one down the shaft from the lid, one of the stem at the
waterline, one of the cable entry, one of the isolation valve. Same four angles
every visit, same order, taken on the inspector's phone and dropped into the
maintenance queue with the finding.

The reason is that the old written findings were not comparable across visits.
"Some silt" from one inspector and "silt present" from another described the same
chamber six months apart and gave no way to say whether it was getting worse. Two
of last year's fouled-stem failures had been described as fine at the previous
inspection, and there was no way to argue with that after the fact.

Rules that come with it:

- **Before touching anything.** The photographs record the state as found. An
  inspector who clears the silt and then photographs has destroyed the record.
- **Four angles, always.** A chamber with nothing wrong still gets four
  photographs. The empty finding is the baseline, and a baseline with gaps in it
  is not a baseline.
- **No annotation in the field.** Notes go in the finding text. An annotated
  photograph cannot be compared against an unannotated one from last cycle.
- **Retention.** Photographs live with the maintenance record, not in the
  telemetry pipeline. They are asset evidence, not sensor data.

Cost is about four minutes per chamber, which on a flood-prone chamber visited
quarterly is under twenty minutes a year. The first cycle under the new rule
finishes in September and will be the first set that can be compared to anything.
