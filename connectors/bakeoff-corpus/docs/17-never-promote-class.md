# Water Infrastructure Innovation Fund, round four awards

The state fund published round four results this week. Nineteen awards totalling
$12.8 million, from 140 applications, the highest application count the fund has
seen.

The telemetry-relevant awards:

- **Brackwater Analytics**, $1.1 million, for a distribution-network sensor
  programme across four small utilities in the northern counties. Their pitch is
  explicitly a shared-services model: one analytics team, four utilities, pooled
  instrumentation procurement. That is a structurally different answer to the same
  problem the eastern ring pilot solves one utility at a time.
- **Kessel County Water**, $780,000, for pressure-zone instrumentation with an
  emphasis on transient detection rather than water quality.
- **Two university programmes**, $340,000 and $295,000, both on machine-learning
  anomaly detection over existing SCADA histories rather than new sensing.

Round four's stated priority was "measurable operational savings within the award
period", which is a change from round three's emphasis on pilots and
demonstrators. The fund's own commentary says the shift came from a review that
found round-two demonstrators had a poor record of continuation once the award
ended, which is the same stranded-asset problem the capital programme people talk
about from the other direction.

Worth knowing about the Brackwater award specifically: their shared-services
model needs the four utilities to agree a common interchange profile, and the
application names TTC-4 as the basis. If they deliver, that is four more TTC-4
deployments in the state and a working example of pooled procurement across small
utilities, which is a thing several people have argued is impossible.

Round five opens in October with the same priority language and, the fund says, a
larger envelope.
