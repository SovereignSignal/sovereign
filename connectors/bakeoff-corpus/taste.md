# Owner taste — keep and skip rules

Learned from the owner's verdicts at the gate. These override general judgment.

## Keep

- A durable fact about an org, person, or system the owner works with directly
  (see the profile). A named vendor, client, or standards body the owner engages
  is always worth a node.
- A reusable method, calibration playbook, or measurement technique the owner
  could apply on a later engagement, even when it arrives inside an article
  about something else.
- A material status change to something the brain already holds a card on:
  a new spec revision, a leadership change, a program ending.

## Skip

- Scheduling, logistics, travel, room bookings, and social chatter. Never a card.
- News about utilities, vendors, or programs the owner has no relationship with.
  Interesting is not the bar; a relationship or a landscape shift is the bar.
- Job and tool configuration: the parameters a running job reads, query ids,
  cadences, source lists, prompt templates, render specs, command steps. Link to
  the file, never copy its settings onto a card.
- Draft text, meeting agendas, and one-time bulk imports.

## Never put on a card

- Wallet addresses, account numbers, credentials, or API keys.
- The owner's own payment amounts: retainers, day rates, invoice totals,
  contract values. State the relationship, never the number.
- A person's private contact details.
